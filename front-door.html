Why this, and not what is there now

The best thing you have is four clicks from the front door.

The room, the lion, the tree growing out of the desk — that is a real idea and almost nobody in this category has one. Keep it. What it is doing wrong is its job: it is the front door, and the front door is where the product should be.

A visitor today scrolls a long cinematic intro, finds card 02, clicks it, reads a panel, and is asked to sign in before they see a single candle move. Everything before that asks to be believed. BarTest does not need to be believed. It can be handed over.

  1. 01

    Six things, all the same size

    Backtest Machine, BarTest Replay, the Factory, the Campus, About and Contact are six identical cards, numbered as if they were a sequence. Two are products, two are not built yet, and one is a contact form. Equal weight tells a first-time visitor nothing about what matters — and the numbering says "read us in order", which is not true of any of them.

  2. 02

    Nothing on the page proves anything

    The pitch is that BarTest settles arguments with real data. The homepage settles nothing: no live number, no real candle, no report. For a product built on evidence, that is the one thing the front page should never be short of.

  3. 03

    The sign-in wall is in front of the demo

    Replay is the reason to buy. It is behind an account. Give away one historical night — no account, no email — and the wall moves to where it earns something: after they have felt it work.

What replaces the six equal cards

Two products, and everything else is a map.

One of these is the flagship and should look like it. The other is the reason the flagship's numbers can be trusted. The rest of the site — the Factory, the Campus, About, Contact — is navigation, and navigation belongs in a row of quiet links, not in the same frame as the thing you sell.
